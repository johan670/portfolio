## Overview

The existing dashboard had grown organically over three years — a graveyard of features no one used and charts that confused rather than clarified. The goal was to redesign it from first principles, guided by how analysts actually worked.

## Discovery

I ran 12 contextual inquiry sessions with analysts across three customer segments. The key insight: users spent 80% of their time on 20% of the features, but the UI treated everything as equally important.

We mapped the full user journey and identified three critical pain points:

1. **Data overload** — too many charts on the default view, no hierarchy
2. **Slow orientation** — users couldn't quickly answer "is everything okay?"
3. **Export friction** — a 7-step process to get data into a spreadsheet

## Design Process

Starting with low-fidelity sketches, I explored three distinct layout directions. After internal critique and a quick unmoderated test, we aligned on a progressive disclosure model: a summary "health" view up front, with drill-down access to detail.

Key decisions:
- Reduced default chart count from 14 to 5 high-signal metrics
- Introduced a status ring to give an at-a-glance system health signal
- Built a one-click CSV export directly into each chart

## Outcome

After launch, time-to-insight dropped 40% based on session recordings. Support tickets related to dashboard confusion fell by 60% in the first month. The redesigned export flow became the most-used feature within two weeks.
